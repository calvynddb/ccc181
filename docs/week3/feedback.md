# Week 3 self-review

## Join the queue — Tsujie (phone and laptop)

> **Works well:** Both frames show the number of open PCs and its update time before the form. Nickname is the main field and phone is optional, so Tsujie can quickly decide whether to join from her phone or laptop. This follows her rule to check availability before travelling.
>
> **Problem:** The frames use different availability wording, and a zero count needs to make clear that joining is still possible. Different labels weaken consistency and may leave Tsujie unsure whether the number means free PCs or stations ready for assignment.
>
> **Change:** Keep the availability-first layout, use **Open PCs** on both sizes, retain **Updated [time]**, and show **No PCs open — join below** when the count is zero. The updated wireframe images include this note.

## My place — Tsujie (phone and laptop)

> **Works well:** The ticket number, text status, and number of people ahead are the visual focus on both sizes. This follows Tsujie’s rule that she must be able to check her place while waiting away from the cafe.
>
> **Problem:** The sketches show only the WAITING state. If staff calls Tsujie, the page also needs to show that state and the assigned PC number; otherwise the most important change is not visible.
>
> **Change:** Keep the layout and annotate both frames so the status remains text and a CALLED ticket shows its assigned PC ID. In the called state, replace the people-ahead message with a clear instruction to go to the assigned PC. The updated wireframe images include the assigned-PC note.

## Staff dashboard — Ethan (phone and laptop)

> **Works well:** The designs show an ordered individual queue, station IDs with FREE/BUSY states, and actions to serve or mark a no-show. This follows Ethan’s rule to scan availability and the waiting list quickly.
>
> **Problem:** The phone’s table makes names, statuses, and actions cramped, while station controls are behind a separate tab. On the laptop, a Call action appears in a queue row as well as in the separate next-ticket panel. That duplication could lead Ethan to call a later ticket or assign a PC inconsistently. These are visibility and error-prevention problems.
>
> **Change:** The revised phone frame uses stacked ticket cards, shows station states on the same screen, and gives the next ticket one large Call next action. The revised laptop frame keeps the ordered table but uses one assignment panel for Call next; Serve and No-show stay with the currently called ticket. This keeps Ethan’s common action prominent and avoids competing call controls.
